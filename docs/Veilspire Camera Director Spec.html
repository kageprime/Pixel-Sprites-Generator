Veilspire Camera Director Spec

Oct 4, 2026 · @kage · Status: draft for review

1. Goal and scope

Build an Auto camera for combat.html that cuts between the eight 45° view angles on combat beats, so fights feel directed, while controls stay predictable and the player can switch it off.

The feel we are chasing: the snap into a new angle at the moment of impact, the way fighting games cut on launches and finishers. Everything else in this spec exists to make that snap safe: it must never hide the fight, steal the player's input, or make anyone sick.

In scope

  • A camera director that decides when to cut and to which angle.
  • Beat triggers (launcher, plunge impact, knockdown, and similar) with a shot preset for each.
  • A readability check so a cut never hides the fight.
  • An input latch so a cut never changes where the player is heading mid-run.
  • An Auto/Manual setting, a manual override, and a debug overlay.

Out of scope for this pass

  • Free-look or camera angles other than the eight 45° steps (the sprites only exist for eight facings).
  • Pitch or height changes. The projection is fixed 2:1 isometric, so a "low angle" shot is not available; a shot differs only by angle, zoom kick, focus and timing.
  • Bespoke per-attack cinematics and finisher cutscenes (later phase, section 11).
  • Characters other than Bram and Cinder, until the others are wired into the page.

2. Research summary and what it changed

Finding Decision it drives
Fighting games already do this. Soul Calibur VI shifts the camera on combo launches, big counter-hits and clashes; Guilty Gear Xrd uses dramatic angles on finishing moves. One fan write-up also reports some players felt queasy from camera pans. Cut on beats, not continuously. Prefer a hard cut to a pan. Ship a way to turn it off.
Unity and Adventure Creator threads describe the same control problem and the same fix: keep the movement direction from the old camera after a cut until the stick is released or its direction changes. The Adventure Creator thread gives concrete release thresholds (input magnitude under 0.1, or direction change of about 5°), and notes that an instant switch carries the movement over but a timed transition makes movement follow the live camera. Latch the held direction in world space. Make the cut instant (0 ticks) so the latch is the only thing governing input.
Cinemachine ClearShot picks the best shot by obstruction checks, and exposes Activate After (wait before switching) and Min Duration (hold before switching again, unless something higher priority fires). State-driven cameras bypass a shot if the state ends before the wait. Our persistence timer (overlap must last 0.5 s), cooldown (1.5 s) and priority override map directly onto these.
Film's 30-degree rule: successive shots of the same subject should differ by at least 30°, or the cut reads as a jump cut. Too big a change disorients. Our 45° step already clears the rule. Limit cuts to 1 to 2 steps (45° to 90°). Never cut to the same angle.
Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117 recommends options to disable camera shake and other camera motion, and to disable automatic camera movement. Auto camera is a toggle. Honour prefers-reduced-motion. Keep it independent from the existing Shake toggle.
A Unity-based game (ChaosBey) went the other way: the camera never turns while a direction is held. Considered and rejected for beat cuts (a cut has to land on the beat). Kept as the fallback behaviour for neutral corrections.

Sources are linked in section 12.

3. What the code does today (constraints)

Found in source/combat.src.html. These shape the design:

  1. Rotation is an ease, not a cut. rotate(d) adds 45 to cam.target; updateCam() eases cam.ang toward it at rate 9. cam.target is never wrapped, so it grows without bound.
  2. Input follows the animated angle. worldMove() maps screen input through cam.ang, so holding a direction during an ease already curves the path. This exists today with Q/E.
  3. Sprite rows follow the animated angle. rowOf(face) uses cam.ang, so during an ease the sprite facing steps through intermediate rows. An instant cut avoids that.
  4. updateCam() runs before the hitstop early-return in simTick(). Camera work continues during hitstop while the sim is frozen. A cut on a hitstop frame is the cleanest cut we can make: nothing moves, input is not consumed, and the player sees the impact pose from the new angle.
  5. Beat hooks already exist. hurt() (kinds light, heavy, launch, air, spike) and enemyLand() (which knows spiked and impact speed) already fire shake, push-in, focus kick and hitstop. The director hooks the same places.
  6. Focus ignores height. updateCam() follows the midpoint of the fighters in x/y only; a launched dummy can leave the frame's centre.
  7. Braziers sit at the four arena corners (BRAZ, tile coords ±4.05) and are depth-sorted into the scene, so a brazier in front of a fighter can cover them.
  8. Slow motion is opt.speed scaling the tick accumulator. Using tick counts (not wall-clock) for all director timers keeps them correct in slow motion.
  9. Small bug to fix on the way: the debug line prints cam.target%360, which can go negative.
  10. Build note: build.py reads combat.src.html and veilspire_iso/ from the current directory and writes out/combat.html. In the zip they are one level apart, so run it from a folder that has both (or adjust the paths). Edit only the source file; the root combat.html is a build output.

4. Design overview

One question drives every decision: from which of the eight angles can the player see the fight best, and is this a moment worth cutting on?

beat fires (hurt / enemyLand)  ──►  queue beat (expires in 6 ticks)
                                         │
directorTick()  (every tick, right after updateCam, before the hitstop return)
   ├─ gates: Auto on? not paused by Q/E? not slow-mo? not locked (air juggle)? cooldown ok?
   ├─ score the 8 angles  (readability, section 6)
   ├─ pick target by the beat's preset (section 5)
   └─ cutTo(angle):  capture input latch → set cam.ang = cam.target = angle (same tick) → kick zoom/flash

A cut is instant: cam.ang and cam.target are set together in one tick, with no tween. Reasons: sprite rows don't step through intermediates, the input latch is the only thing governing movement, and it matches how cuts are used in film and in the fighting-game references above.

Manual Q/E keeps its current eased rotation.

5. Beats and shot presets

Each beat is a row of data, not code. Priority decides who wins when two beats arrive together or when one wants to bypass the cooldown.

Beat Hook Cut timing Angle rule Existing FX reused Priority
Plunge slam enemyLand() when spiked First frame of the 9-tick hitstop Best side-on within ±90°; forced cut allowed (see below) push .12, focusKick 1, ring 3
Launcher connects hurt(), kind launch, first hit when dummy was grounded First frame of the 6-tick hitstop Best side-on within ±90°; forced cut allowed push .08 3
Heavy launch (attack3) hurt(), kind heavy Hitstop Only if current view quality is under 0.4 push .10 2
Big knockdown (non-spike) enemyLand() when impact speed over 420 Hitstop Only if current view quality is under 0.4 existing 2
Neutral correction Overlap persists 0.5 s while Bram is grounded and Cinder is idle, stunned or getting up Next tick, no hitstop One step toward the best angle, only if clearly better none 1
Light hits, air hits, bounce re-lands, whiffs n/a No cut n/a existing shake only n/a

Forced cut. A hero beat (priority 3) is allowed to cut even if the current angle is already fine, because the cut itself is the effect. It picks the highest-scoring angle that is not the current one, within 1 to 2 steps, and only if that angle's readability is at least 0.6. In practice, from an ideal side-on view this swings to a neighbouring step (about 0.71 readability), which reads like film coverage rather than a mistake.

Lockout during air combos. While Cinder is airborne and not spiked (E.state==='air' && !E.spiked), no beat or correction may cut. The launcher cut happens on the hit that starts the juggle; the lock begins right after it. The plunge slam is the one beat that fires as the lock ends (the dummy lands, E.state becomes down).

Queue expiry. A beat that cannot be served within 6 ticks is dropped. A late cut feels random.

6. Readability check

For each of the eight candidate angles θ, compute where Cinder appears relative to Bram on screen, using the same transform as proj():

dx = E.x - P.x ; dy = E.y - P.y
rx = dx*cosθ - dy*sinθ                       // horizontal screen offset
ry = (dx*sinθ + dy*cosθ) * 0.5 - (E.z - P.z) // vertical screen offset (squash and height)

Score components, each normalised to 0..1:

  • side = |rx| / hypot(dx, dy). 1 means the line between the fighters is horizontal on screen; 0 means one stands behind the other. Two angles 180° apart are equally good; the turn-cost term below picks the nearer.
  • overlap penalty: 1 when the sprite boxes intersect, i.e. |rx| < W and |ry| < H, fading to 0 over a margin. Start with W ≈ 28 px and H ≈ 44 px at zoom 1 (sprite cell is 96 px; tune by eye). The E.z term matters: a juggled dummy rises out from behind Bram.
  • brazier penalty: for each brazier that would be drawn in front of either fighter (larger depth), penalise if its screen position is within about 40 px of that fighter.
  • edge penalty: either fighter projected within about 36 px of the viewport edge (use VW/VH, which change on narrow layouts).
  • turn cost: small penalty per step from the current angle, so near ties favour the shorter turn.
quality(θ) = 0.55*side + 0.25*(1 - overlap) + 0.10*(1 - brazier) + 0.10*(1 - edge) - 0.05*steps(θ)

These weights are starting values. Debug overlay (section 9) exists so they can be tuned by feel.

Hysteresis. For non-forced moves, the candidate must beat the current angle by at least 0.25. Persistence. A neutral correction needs the current view to be bad (quality under 0.4, or overlap true) for 30 consecutive ticks.

7. Input latch

Movement is camera-relative, so a cut while a direction is held would swing the player's heading.

  • On cutTo(), if there is held movement input, store latch = { ang: <the cam angle before the cut>, dir: <raw screen-space input angle> }.
  • worldMove() uses latch.ang instead of cam.ang while a latch exists. The player keeps travelling in the same world direction.
  • Release the latch when any of these happens: raw input magnitude drops below 0.1 (no keys held; touch stick uses its existing 0.15 dead zone), raw input direction changes by 20° or more from latch.dir, resetAll() runs, or the window loses focus.
  • Further cuts while latched do not replace latch.ang; the original mapping persists until release (matching the carry-over behaviour in the references).
  • The bot demo returns world-space movement directly, so it is unaffected.
  • Open question (section 10): apply the same latch to manual Q/E turns. Recommended yes, since it fixes the existing veer.

The 20° threshold is my choice for keyboard and analog use; the 5° in the reference is tighter than a thumb stick can hold. Tune in playtest.

8. Controls and settings

  • Auto camera toggle button in the #opts bar (data-o="autocam"), backed by opt.autocam. The existing generic toggle() wiring handles it with no new handler.
  • C key toggles it. Add a line to the controls list.
  • Small AUTO badge beside the Q/E buttons: on, paused (shows a short countdown), or off. Needed on touch, where there is no key hint.
  • Default: on, except when prefers-reduced-motion: reduce matches, then off. Persist the choice in localStorage inside try/catch (it may be unavailable); the page must work without it.
  • Manual override: Q, E, or the on-screen rotate buttons pause Auto for 4 s (240 ticks), cancel any queued beat, and extend on each press. When the pause ends, wait 1 s before the first auto-cut.
  • Slow motion (T): no auto-cuts while opt.speed < 1, so frames can be judged undisturbed. A setting can override this later.
  • Reduced motion: the cut flash (below) is off, and zoom kick is halved.
  • Cut flash: a 2-tick, low-alpha screen flash on hero cuts to mask the swap. Because cuts are at least 36 ticks apart, flashes stay well under three per second.
  • URL flag ?auto=0|1 for testing, next to the existing ?demo=1 and ?debug=1.

9. Debug overlay

Extend the existing frame-data overlay (F key):

  • Line of text: cam 135° auto on | paused 2.1s | locked:air cd 0.8s last: launcher → 135° q.72.
  • A small 8-spoke compass showing each angle's quality, with the current angle marked.
  • The Bram→Cinder line drawn in the arena, and the latched world heading as an arrow while a latch is active.
  • A ring buffer of the last 8 cuts with beat, angle change, quality and tick, so you can tell which cuts earned their place.
  • Per-beat on/off switches in dir.beats (reachable from the console via window.__vs) so beats can be A/B tested by feel. Fix the negative-modulo print here too.

10. Open questions

  1. Latch manual Q/E too? (Recommended: yes.)
  2. Should Auto default to on for everyone, or opt-in? (Recommended: on, with reduced-motion exception.)
  3. Is a 4 s manual pause right, or should any manual turn disable Auto until re-enabled?
  4. Do you want the cut flash at all, or a clean hard cut? Easy to remove.
  5. Should the focus follow include height (item 6 in section 3) in this pass? It helps launch shots read; it is a small change.

11. Later phases

  • Per-attack camera presets (signature angle and zoom per move).
  • A short "cinematic" mode for big finishers: longer hold, closer zoom, brief slow-down.
  • Brazier-aware nudging near walls beyond the simple penalty.
  • Extra characters once wired in.

12. Implementation plan

All edits in source/combat.src.html, then rebuild.

Phase 0: groundwork

  • Normalise angles: wrap cam.ang/cam.target after a settle; shortest-path helper; fix the debug print.
  • Add the input latch and wire worldMove(); apply to manual Q/E per the open question.
  • Acceptance: with Auto off, behaviour matches today except the latch.

Phase 1: director core, two beats

  • dir state, directorBeat(name), directorTick() placed right after updateCam() in simTick(), cutTo().
  • Wire launcher (in hurt()) and plunge slam (in enemyLand()).
  • Gates: air lockout, cooldown (90 ticks, hard floor 36 for priority override), Q/E pause.
  • Auto toggle button, C key, badge.

Phase 2: readability and remaining beats

  • Quality scoring, hysteresis, forced-cut rule, brazier and edge penalties.
  • Heavy launch, big knockdown, neutral correction with persistence timer.
  • Slow-motion gate and reduced-motion default.

Phase 3: debug and tuning

  • Overlay, compass, cut log, per-beat switches.
  • Tune the numbers in the table below by playing the bot demo and by hand.

13. Acceptance tests

Run through window.__vs (simTick, press, keys, cam) so they are scriptable without rendering.

  1. Regression: Auto off, 10 min of bot demo, identical camera behaviour to the current build.
  2. Cut timing: every beat cut occurs on a tick where hitstop > 0; cam.ang === cam.target on the same tick; the step is 1 or 2 (45° or 90°).
  3. Cooldown: no two cuts closer than 36 ticks; no two within 90 ticks unless the later one has higher priority.
  4. Air lockout: zero cuts while E.state==='air' && !E.spiked.
  5. Latch: hold d, trigger a cut, then confirm Bram's world velocity direction is unchanged (within 1°) until d is released or the direction changes 20° or more; afterwards movement follows the new camera.
  6. Readability: across 500 random fighter placements, any non-forced cut raises quality by at least 0.25; forced cuts never go below 0.6.
  7. Override: after pressing E, no auto-cut for 240 ticks; after that, none for a further 60.
  8. Slow motion: no cuts at opt.speed 0.5 or 0.25.
  9. Reduced motion: with the media query forced, Auto defaults off and no flash appears.
  10. Soak: 5 min of bot demo at accelerated ticks: no NaN, no stuck latch, cut rate within a sane band (start guess: 6 to 14 cuts per minute during combos). Cuts per minute is the key "too much" signal.
  11. Cost: director tick under 0.2 ms.
